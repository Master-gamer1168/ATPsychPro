## What the data show (design inferred from the PDF)

- **N = 32** participants: 4 sheets × 8. *Assumed to be 32 different people.* The PDF numbers them P1–P8 on every sheet.
- **IV:** effort label, i.e. whether a piece was described as taking **1 week** (high effort) or **2 days** (low effort).
- **DVs:** **liking** and **quality** ratings.
- **Design:** **repeated measures**. Every participant rated two stimuli, **SoR** and **CYFI**, one under each label.
- **Counterbalanced:** sheets 1 and 4 labelled SoR as "1 week"; sheets 2 and 3 labelled CYFI as "1 week".
- **Difference** = 1 week − 2 days. Positive means the high-effort-labelled piece was rated higher.

### To confirm against your workbook
1. What **SoR** and **CYFI** are (paintings? poems?), and their full titles for the Materials section.
2. The **rating scale**, e.g. 1–10? Ratings range from 2 to 9; whether 10 was possible matters for the ceiling-effect question.
3. Whether the 4 sheets are **4 separate groups** (assumed here), and whether anything differed between sessions. Sheet 1 is the odd one out; see below.
4. Whether your class planned a **chi-square** on something specific. See "Which test?" below.
5. The anchor study's citation. The effort heuristic is usually attributed to **Kruger et al. (2004)**; check it against your workbook.

## Which test?

- **Wilcoxon signed-rank is the match for this design:** repeated measures, ordinal ratings, a directional hypothesis (one-tailed). Report it as the main inferential test.
- **The chi-square in RESULTS.md** counts how many participants rated the high-effort piece higher vs lower (ties excluded) and compares that with a 50/50 split. It's there because you asked for chi-square values. Be aware that chi-square is designed for **nominal frequency data from independent observations**; here it throws away the size of each difference. If your teacher or workbook specified chi-square, use it and justify it as "participants' preferences were categorised as nominal data (higher/lower)". Otherwise use the Wilcoxon.

### Reporting sentences (adapt; these follow the guide's Results format)

> A one-tailed Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used because the hypothesis was directional, the design was repeated measures and the ratings were treated as ordinal. For liking, *T* = 30 (critical value = 84, *N* = 26 after 6 tied pairs were excluded, *p* < .01); for quality, *T* = 39 (critical value = 140, *N* = 32, *p* < .01). The null hypothesis was therefore rejected for both measures.

## Hooks for the Discussion (revised structure)

These are observations from the data, not written paragraphs. Check them against [`../../sources/discussion-structure-revised.md`](../../sources/discussion-structure-revised.md).

- **¶1 outliers / unexpected:**
  - **Sheet 1 is weaker than the others.** Mean quality difference is 0.38, against 1.13–1.38 elsewhere. It holds **all 3 quality reversals** (S1-P2, P3, P8) and 2 of the 4 liking reversals. Its per-sheet tests aren't significant; all other sheets are.
  - **S1-P7** gave the single lowest rating (quality = 2).
  - The **low-effort quality ratings have the widest spread** (*SD* 1.50, range 2–9).
- **¶2 effect size:** every individual difference is between −1 and +2 scale points. The shift is consistent (91% of participants rated quality higher) but modest (~1 point).
- **¶3 limitation ideas (internal validity / reliability, tied to the numbers):**
  - *Demand characteristics from the repeated-measures design:* each participant saw both labels, so the contrast "1 week vs 2 days" may have revealed the aim. This could produce a small, uniform "polite" bump of 1–2 points, which matches the tight range of differences. Concrete fix: an independent-measures version, or effort information embedded among filler details (artist name, materials).
  - *Inconsistent testing conditions:* sheet 1's weaker effect may reflect something about that session (instructions, timing, order of presentation). Concrete fix: a standardized script and presentation order, run in one session format.
  - *Halo / overlapping DVs:* liking and quality correlate strongly (Spearman ρ = .77), so the two ratings may not be measuring separate judgements. Concrete fix: define each rating on the response sheet with anchor descriptions.
  - *Possible ceiling:* depends on the scale (see "To confirm" above).
- **¶4 generalisation:**
  - *Mirrors well:* judging unfamiliar art or handmade goods where the effort or time is stated (craft fairs, online listings stating "hand-made over 40 hours").
  - *Would change:* when people have expertise or other quality cues (price, reviews, the artist's reputation), or when the effort claim isn't credible.
