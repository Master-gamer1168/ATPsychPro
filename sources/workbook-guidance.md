## Finalizing the Paper (p. 10)

Now that your experiment is complete, your statistical analysis is finished, and your discussion is written, it is time to assemble and polish your final research report.

There is an important distinction between writing order and final assembly order:

**Writing Order**: You write your Introduction first, then your Method, conduct the study, write the Results and Discussion, and write the Abstract last.

**Final Assembly Order**: The final document follows standard APA layout, where the Title Page is Page 1, the Abstract is Page 2, and the Introduction begins on Page 3.

## Writing Your Abstract (Written Last, Placed on Page 2) (p. 11)

The abstract is a concise, single-paragraph overview (120–150 words summarizing your entire investigation. It allows readers to quickly understand the background, method, core findings, and significance of your study.

**APA Formatting Rules for the Abstract**
- Appears on its own separate page directly after the Title Page (Page 2).
- Center the bolded section label Abstract at the top of the page.
- Write as a single double-spaced block paragraph. Do not indent the first line.
- Keep within 120–150 words. (Note: The abstract does not count toward your final 1,800–2,200 word count.

**Abstract Checklist**
Ensure your abstract includes 1–2 sentences for each of the following components:
- [ ] Context & Aim: State the theoretical framework or original study being replicated, along with your primary research aim or hypothesis.
- [ ] Participants: Report the total sample size (N, relevant demographics (e.g., Grade 11 high school students, and the sampling method used.
- [ ] Methodology: State the experimental design (e.g., independent measures or repeated measures, operationalized IV and DV, and the core experimental task.
- [ ] Findings: State the key descriptive statistics (means and standard deviations for both conditions and inferential test outcome (test used, calculated value, and significance level.
- [ ] Conclusions & Real-World Application: State whether the research hypothesis was supported and summarize the theoretical relevance or practical real-world application of your findings.

**Abstract Writing Template & Sentence Starters**
- *Context & Aim*: This experiment replicated [Original Study / Theory] to investigate the effect of [IV] on [DV]...
- *Participants & Design*: A sample of [N] high school participants was recruited using [sampling method] in an experimental [independent measures / repeated measures] design...
- *Procedure*: Participants completed [brief description of experimental task] under either [Condition A] or [Condition B]...
- *Results*: Descriptive statistics revealed that [Condition A mean/SD] compared to [Condition B mean/SD]. An inferential [Name of Test] indicated that this difference was [statistically significant / not statistically significant] ([test symbol] = [value], p [</>] .05...
- *Conclusion & Application*: These findings [support / refute] the hypothesis and demonstrate that [state practical real-world application or theoretical implication].

## Title Page (Page 1) (p. 11)

Your title page must follow APA formatting conventions and clearly identify your study.

**Title Page Checklist**
- [ ] Running Head / Page Number: Page number 1 positioned in the top-right header.
- [ ] Descriptive Title: Centered, bolded, and positioned in the upper half of the page. The title must clearly communicate what was investigated, including the operationalized independent and dependent variables (e.g., The Effect of Word Concreteness on Immediate Free Recall in High School Students).
- [ ] Student Information: Student names and block.
- [ ] Course Information: AT Psychology, Singapore American School.
- [ ] Submission Date: Month and year of submission.
- [ ] Word Count: State the final word count of your report body (excluding title page, abstract, references, data tables, and appendices).

## In-Text Citations & References (p. 12)

To meet disciplinary conventions, all background research, replication sources, and external evidence must be cited in standard APA format (7th Edition).

**In-Text Citation Checklist**
- [ ] Parenthetical citations format correctly: (Author, Year) or (Author & Author, Year).
- [ ] For three or more authors, use the first author followed by et al.: (Author et al., Year).
- [ ] Narrative citations introduce sources naturally: According to Loftus and Palmer (1974)...
- [ ] Every factual claim, theoretical model, and past study mentioned in your Introduction and Discussion has a corresponding citation.

**Reference List Checklist**
- [ ] Starts on its own new page immediately after your Discussion section.
- [ ] Centered, bolded heading: References.
- [ ] Alphabetized by the first author's last name.
- [ ] Formatted using a hanging indent (first line flush left, subsequent lines indented 0.5 inches).
- [ ] Double-spaced throughout with no extra blank lines between entries.
- [ ] Every entry in the reference list matches an in-text citation in the body of the paper (and vice versa).

## Compiling Appendices (p. 12)

Appendices contain the raw materials, instruments, and evidence needed to verify your procedure and enable replication.

**Appendix Formatting Rules**
- Begin each appendix on a separate page following your References list.
- Label each appendix with a capital letter and title at the top, bolded and centered (e.g., Appendix A: Standardized Participant Instructions).
- Refer to every appendix directly within the body text of your report (e.g., "Participants signed an informed consent form (see Appendix B) before testing began.").

**Required Appendices Inventory**
- [ ] Appendix A: Materials Used in the Experiment (Copies of word lists, image sets, questionnaires, test sheets, or stimulus slides used in both conditions).
- [ ] Appendix B: Standardized Instructions (The verbatim script read aloud to all participants to ensure experimental control).
- [ ] Appendix C: Informed Consent Form (The document signed by participants or parents detailing voluntary participation, confidentiality, and right to withdraw).
- [ ] Appendix D: Debriefing Statement (The script and information sheet provided to participants immediately following the experiment explaining the true nature of the study).
- [ ] Appendix E: Raw Data & Statistical Calculations (The raw data table showing scores for all individual participants, followed by step-by-step inferential test calculation work).

## Final Report Layout & Assembly Checklist (p. 12)

Before submitting your final PDF, verify that your document is ordered sequentially and adheres to all structural guidelines:

**Page Sequence Order**
- Page 1: Title Page (Title, names, course, date, word count)
- Page 2: Abstract (Single unindented block paragraph, 120–150 words)
- Pages 3+:
  - Introduction (Theoretical context, synthesized literature, gap justification, hypotheses)
  - Methodology (Design, participants, materials, step-by-step replicable procedure)
  - Results (Descriptive statistics narrative, summary table, graph, inferential statistics outcome)
  - Discussion (Evaluation against theory, limitations, improvements, real-world implications)
  - References (Alphabetical, APA 7th edition, hanging indent)
  - Appendices (Labeled A through E in order of appearance in the text)

## Document Mechanics Checklist (p. 13)

- [ ] Font: Standard academic font (Times New Roman 12pt, Arial 11pt, or Calibri 11pt).
- [ ] Spacing & Margins: Double-spaced throughout with 1-inch (2.54 cm) margins on all sides.
- [ ] Page Numbers: Numbered consecutively in the upper right-hand corner of the header starting on page 1.
- [ ] Word Count: The main body (Introduction through Discussion) falls within the 1,800–2,200 word range. (Title page, abstract, tables, figures, references, and appendices are excluded from the word count.)
- [ ] Rubric Verification: Check that your Discussion explicitly justifies real-world implications and your Introduction synthesizes diverse perspectives to meet the highest performance criteria.

## Phase instructions (pp. 15–20): key requirements (editorial summary, not verbatim)

Full text is in the PDF. Points that affect the report:
- **Check-In 3 (Workbook p. 10 conference)** asks you to demonstrate three things:
  1. **Evaluation depth**: limitations about methodological and design validity, "rather than superficial issues like 'we were tired'".
  2. **Literature synthesis**: how specifically your findings confirm, extend or contradict the anchor study.
  3. **Real-world implications**: what practical, actionable application your data supports.
- **To score "Meets with Distinction"**, the Discussion must justify the real-world implications of your findings.
- **Data Analysis Lab:** calculate condition means and SDs, build an APA-formatted graph with clear axes and titles, calculate the inferential statistic, and decide whether p < .05. The teacher verifies this before you write the narrative.
- **Pilot (Peer Pilot Swap):** watch for demand characteristics, evaluation apprehension, confusing instructions, pacing issues, and floor/ceiling effects. Record what went wrong and what you changed on Workbook p. 7.
- **Ethics:** the six core BPS/APA principles are informed consent, protection from harm, deception limits, debriefing, right to withdraw, and confidentiality.
- **Peer audit (Dec 2/3):** body within 1,800–2,200 words; every citation ↔ reference; Appendices A–E labelled, titled and cited in the text.
- Several steps refer to sections of the class *Research Methods Guide*, which isn't in this repo:
  - Section 2: Hypotheses & Operationalization; Participant Biases & Controls
  - Section 3: Experimental Allocation Designs
  - Section 6: Sampling Methods
  - Section 8: Quantitative Quality Criteria
  - Section 9: Levels of Measurement; Quantitative Data, Statistics & Inferential Testing
  - Section 10: Human Ethics Standards
