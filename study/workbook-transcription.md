## Page 2: Background Research

*Theory or Model:* Heuristics

### *Summary of Theory/Model: Key Points* (*Write in note form.*)
- Heuristics are mental shortcuts that tend to work but can produce predictable errors.
  - ↳ In the study: a hard question is replaced w/ an easier one (attribute substitution).
- Heuristics tend to have stronger effects when you have less info (ambiguity).
- Effort heuristic based on availability heuristic: effort is used to judge quality.
- NOT cognitive dissonance (judging own work).

### *Aims and Variables: Our Study*

| | |
|---|---|

## Page 3: Hypotheses, Design, Sampling

*Research Hypothesis (H₁):* HS students will give an overall higher rating in likeability & quality (on a scale from 1–11) to a song they are told took 20 hrs to make than a song they were told took 2 hrs to create.

*Null Hypothesis (H₀):* There is no significant difference in quality & likeability ratings between the song they were told took 20 hrs & the 2 hr song.

*Our Design:* Repeated measures + counterbalancing

*Explanation of why we chose this design:*
Very similar to experiment 2 in the original study. Each participant hears two songs where one is said to have taken 2 hrs & the other took 20 hrs to produce. We use repeated measures because it helps control/reduce variation between subjects. Each subject is compared to themselves, unlike that in an independent measures. We counterbalance to account for any confounding variables. If one song was always said to be the 20 hr one, it might result in higher ratings simply b/c it's an objectively better/more likeable song. Swapping the label means we can control for this. We also change the order of which is heard first b/c it's plausible it affects people's perceived ratings (e.g., the 2nd song is rated differently b/c of boredom, practice, etc).

*Our Sampling Method:* Convenience sampling of advisory groups (cluster if we can select advisories @ random)

*Explanation of how we will get our sample:*
- Ask advisors @ SAS if we can run the study during advisory.
- Test each advisory. All students listen to the song @ the same time.
- Half the students are told the first song took 2 hrs, other half told 20 hrs.
- Target: 4 advisories (N ≈ 40 [?])

*Explanation of why we chose this method:*
1. Practicality: b/c we have a short time window, we can gather enough data using advisories.
2. Advisories are, presumably, random. Students have varying backgrounds, so a more varied sample.
3. Original study used a similar approach (also convenience).
4. Only sampling advisories whose advisor agrees. Testing in a big group means subjects may influence each other. Convenience also limits generalization. ← *(margin arrow: "Limitations")*

## Page 5: Procedures, Materials

### *Notes on procedures*
- Agree w/ an advisory & advisor about a time.
- Introduce ourselves & the study. Hand out consent form.
- Participants sit apart & be quiet. AT psych students can join but their data won't be used.
- Alternate versions of handouts given. Each sheet should have participant numbers.
- Read instructions.
- Participants flip paper; papers have a lot of additional info.
- Play song 1, students rate. Repeat for song 2.
- After collecting, debrief.
- Thank everyone.
