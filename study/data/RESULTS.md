## 3. Wilcoxon signed-rank test (N = 32): the main inferential test

Repeated measures (each participant rated both songs, one under each effort label) with ordinal rating data → **Wilcoxon signed-rank**. H₁ is directional, so the test is **one-tailed**. Significant if *T* ≤ critical value and the difference is in the predicted direction.

| Measure | n (non-zero d) | Ties dropped | Σ ranks + | Σ ranks − | ***T*** | Critical *T* (.05, one-tailed) | Critical *T* (.01, one-tailed) | Result | Rank-biserial *r* | z (tie-corrected) | one-tailed *p* ≈ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Combined DV** (mean of liking + quality) | 29 | 3 | 417 | 18 | **18** | 140 | 110 | **Significant, p < .01** | 0.92 | -4.40 | < .00001 |
| Liking (likeability) | 26 | 6 | 321 | 30 | **30** | 110 | 84 | **Significant, p < .01** | 0.83 | -3.81 | .00007 |
| Quality | 32 | 0 | 489 | 39 | **39** | 175 | 140 | **Significant, p < .01** | 0.85 | -4.48 | < .00001 |

Critical values are exact (Wilcoxon null distribution, computed by `tools/stats.py`, which reproduces published tables). Many |d| values are tied, which exact tables don't account for; the tie-corrected z gives the same verdict in every case. Rank-biserial *r* runs from −1 to +1, and values ≥ .5 are conventionally a large effect.

## 4. Chi-square goodness of fit on direction (+ vs −), with exact sign test

This tests whether more participants rated the high-effort song higher than the 50/50 split chance predicts. Ties are excluded, df = 1, and expected frequency = (number of + and −) ÷ 2. **Note:** chi-square is designed for nominal frequency data from independent observations. For paired ratings, the Wilcoxon (section 3) uses more of the information, and the **sign test** is the exact version of this same comparison.

| Measure | Observed + | Observed − | Expected each | χ² | df | Critical χ² (.05, non-directional) | Critical χ² (.05 one-tailed) | Result | Sign test exact one-tailed *p* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Combined DV** (mean of liking + quality) | 27 | 2 | 14.5 | **21.55** | 1 | 3.841 | 2.706 | Significant, p < .01 | < .00001 |
| Liking (likeability) | 22 | 4 | 13 | **12.46** | 1 | 3.841 | 2.706 | Significant, p < .01 | .00027 |
| Quality | 29 | 3 | 16 | **21.12** | 1 | 3.841 | 2.706 | Significant, p < .01 | < .00001 |

χ² working: χ² = Σ (O − E)² ÷ E. Critical values for df = 1: p < .10 two-tailed (= .05 one-tailed): 2.706; p < .05 two-tailed: 3.841; p < .02 two-tailed (= .01 one-tailed): 5.412; p < .01 two-tailed: 6.635.

## 5. Between-student analysis: same song, different label (Mann-Whitney U)

The workbook (p. 6) notes "Random treatment assignment → between-student analysis". This holds the **song constant** and compares students who were told it took 1 week with students who were told it took 2 days. Different people are in each group, so it's an **independent-measures** comparison → **Mann-Whitney U**, one-tailed (1-week group predicted higher). Useful as a check: it doesn't depend on any one participant comparing the two songs side by side.

| Song | Measure | Told 1 week: n, *M* (*SD*) | Told 2 days: n, *M* (*SD*) | *U* | Critical *U* (.05, one-tailed) | Critical *U* (.01, one-tailed) | Result | z (tie-corrected) | one-tailed *p* ≈ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SoR | Combined | 16, 6.91 (1.16) | 16, 6.16 (0.96) | **77** | 83 | 66 | **Significant, p < .05** | -1.94 | .02598 |
| SoR | Liking | 16, 6.94 (1.18) | 16, 6.19 (1.11) | **84** | 83 | 66 | Not significant | -1.71 | .04363 |
| SoR | Quality | 16, 6.88 (1.31) | 16, 6.12 (0.96) | **80** | 83 | 66 | **Significant, p < .05** | -1.86 | .03125 |
| CYFI | Combined | 16, 7.22 (1.05) | 16, 6.00 (1.62) | **69.5** | 83 | 66 | **Significant, p < .05** | -2.23 | .01300 |
| CYFI | Liking | 16, 7.12 (1.26) | 16, 6.00 (1.51) | **72.5** | 83 | 66 | **Significant, p < .05** | -2.13 | .01639 |

U = n1·n2 + n(n + 1) ÷ 2 − R. Each U was cross-checked by directly counting pairs. Critical values are exact for n1 = n2 = 16. Each comparison uses only one rating per student, so it has less power than the repeated-measures Wilcoxon.

- **Every individual liking or quality difference is between −1 and +2.** The shift is consistent but modest.
